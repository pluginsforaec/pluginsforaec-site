ARQO Stair+

Click the foot and the top (a third click for a landing or winders): the risers, the going and 2R+G come from the finished levels, checked against the rules. The floors are read: the rough flight stands on the slab under its marble, granite, wood, ceramic or epoxy finish, the slab above is opened where headroom would fall under 2.10 m and the headroom is measured. Simple, Slab, ZigZag, Steps, Treads, Stringers, Mono, Cantilever, Steel, Ramp and Spiral; landings with their beam; balanced winders; parts named and tagged for sections; one group, one Undo.

How to use it

  1. Start ARQO Stair+ and press Tab to choose Simple, Slab, ZigZag, Steps, Treads, Stringers, Mono, Cantilever, Steel, Ramp or Spiral; on the card choose the Plan (Straight, With landing, Winders, Arc, Three flights), the Line (Centre, Left edge, Right edge), the Finish of a concrete stair, the Slab opening (Cut, Show, Leave), the railing and its side.
  2. Type a width, or a number of risers, if you need them.
  3. Click the foot: where the first riser stands (a spiral: its centre).
  4. Move the mouse; the card shows risers, going, 2R+G and the rule checks.
  5. Click the top: the edge the stair arrives at (a spiral: a point on the top floor's edge — the radius and where it arrives).
  6. With landing: click the foot, then the landing (where the stair rests or turns), then the top. The top ahead makes a straight stair with a landing, to a side an L, back beside the first flight a U.
  7. Winders: the same three clicks, the middle one the corner where it turns; the turn is made of balanced treads instead of a landing.
  8. Arc: the same three clicks, the middle one a point on the curve; the stair follows the circle through the three points.
  9. Three flights: the foot, the first landing, then the top beside flight 1 (back the way it came). Escape stair: the top escape door's threshold, then the ground beside the façade.

Good to know

  • The number of risers comes from a 175 mm riser unless you type it; the last step ends level with the top edge.
  • Checks: riser at most 180 mm, going at least 270 mm, 2R+G between 600 and 650 mm. A stair that fails is still drawn and the report says why.
  • The top must be higher than the foot. Clicked straight above the foot, the going is 280 mm.
  • Simple, Slab and ZigZag are one closed solid; Steps and Treads are one body per step inside the stair's group.
  • The railing: an oak handrail 900 above the nosings on steel posts, with bars, glass, rods or a solid upstand; both sides, the left or the right. A spiral turns oak wedges round a steel column, its walking line (60 % of the radius) 230 a tread, its handrail a helix; anticlockwise or clockwise on the card.
  • Type and width are remembered. One group, drawn in the open context; one Undo removes it.
  • Landings: both flights share one going so the stair arrives at the top you clicked; the landing moves to the nearest whole step. It is at least the width and 1000 mm (a ramp: 1500), and its open edges get a guard 1100 high. A U turns round a 100 mm well.
  • Line: Left edge or Right edge puts the stair beside the line you click — draw along a wall, or round a wall's corner for an L. No railing and no guard on the wall's side.
  • Ramp: one slope from the foot to the top. Checks: at most 1:12 (8.3 %), the slope recommended for its height (8 % up to 150 mm, 7 % to 500, 6 % to 1000, then 5 %), and a landing every 760 mm of rise — draw it With landing.
  • Click finished levels (the floor's top). The floors under the foot and at the top are read: the rough flight stands on the slab under the floor's build-up and its treads lie under the finish, so every finished riser comes out equal. The card shows the first rough riser.
  • Finish (concrete stairs): Marble or Granite (tread 30 + riser 20 on a 20 mortar bed), Wood, Ceramic, Epoxy or None. No nosing projection; along a wall a raked skirting.
  • Slab opening: where the walking line comes within 2.10 m of the slab above, every layer of that floor is cut in place — 50 mm wider on a free side, over landings and a U's well — in the same Undo as the stair. Show only draws it; Leave keeps the slab. The card and the report give the headroom.
  • A Slab stair: the waist is span/25 (at least 120); a turning landing gets a 25/50 beam; a slab stair on no floor gets a footing.
  • Winders: equal goings on the walking line at mid-width, the inner edge at least 100 mm, 8 or more treads round the turn; Balanced, or Single point for an L.
  • Stringers, Mono and Steel stand on steel; Cantilever comes out of a wall — set the Line to the wall's edge. Open stairs check the gap between treads against a 100 mm sphere.
  • Parts are named, with their materials, on the tags ARQO Stair · Structure, · Finish, · Railing and · Opening, so Section Sheet hatches concrete, mortar and stone apart.
  • Arc: equal goings on the walking line, so the risers are radial; the inner edge needs room and the turn stays under a full circle. Simple, Slab, ZigZag, Steps and Treads; the finish, the railings and the slab opening as on any stair.
  • Closed string: timber strings with the treads and risers housed in them. Cut string: laser-cut steel plates with the treads on the cuts, and a timber wall string on a wall side. Railing Timber spindles: newels, a 75 × 50 rail and two spindles a tread (gap under 100). Timber stairs are for inside a dwelling only (PAİY 31(1)a).
  • Escape stair (BYKHY 41–42): galvanised steel, a stacked U a storey with a floor landing at each door, guards 1100, R ≤ 175, G ≥ 250, 3–17 risers a flight, ≤ 3.00 m between landings; refused over 21.50 m. A straight stair over 18 risers gets a landing half way.
